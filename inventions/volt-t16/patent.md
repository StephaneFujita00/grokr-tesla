# Manufacturing torque verification system for suspension lateral link fasteners

## Abstract
A manufacturing station verifies proper torque on front suspension lateral link fasteners during Model Y assembly. A reaction torque sensor on the nutrunner, combined with a sub-frame mounted strain gauge array, confirms clamp load before the vehicle exits the station. Data logs prevent release of vehicles with insufficient torque.

## Problem
During sub-frame to body marriage, one or more lateral link fasteners may receive insufficient torque. Subsequent road loads allow the link to loosen or detach from the sub-frame, altering wheel alignment and vehicle control.

## Prior art
- CN118144474A discloses a multi-link rear axle assembly structure but provides no in-line torque verification after fastener installation.
- US5332255A describes a front suspension with torque springs yet lacks any sensor-based clamp load confirmation on lateral link bolts.

## Summary of the invention
The invention adds a dual-sensor verification step at the suspension marriage station. A servo nutrunner applies final torque while a reaction sensor measures applied torque and a sub-frame strain gauge array confirms resulting preload. Both values must match calibrated thresholds within 3 percent before the station controller releases the vehicle.

## Claims
1. A manufacturing verification method comprising: installing a lateral link fastener (12) through a sub-frame boss (14) into a lateral link eye (16) with a servo nutrunner (18); measuring reaction torque with sensor (20) mounted on the nutrunner housing; measuring local strain with at least three strain gauges (22) bonded to the sub-frame boss outer wall; comparing both torque and strain values to stored calibration curves; and inhibiting vehicle release unless both values lie within 3 percent of target preload corresponding to 120 Nm torque.
2. The method of claim 1 further comprising logging the torque and strain pair to a vehicle-specific record in the plant MES database.
3. The method of claim 1 wherein the strain gauges are wired to a station PLC that performs the comparison within 200 ms after torque application ceases.
4. The method of claim 1 further comprising, on out-of-tolerance detection, commanding the nutrunner to apply an additional 10 Nm increment and re-measure.
5. The method of claim 1 wherein the calibration curve is generated by applying known torques to sample sub-frame assemblies instrumented with ultrasonic bolt stretch measurement.
6. The method of claim 1 wherein failure to meet tolerance after two re-torque attempts triggers an alert to the station operator and halts the line segment.

## Detailed description
The sub-frame (10) carries the lateral link fastener (12) through boss (14) into the lateral link eye (16). Servo nutrunner (18) engages the fastener head. Reaction torque sensor (20) is rigidly attached to the nutrunner housing and measures torque reacted into the station fixture. Three strain gauges (22) are bonded at 120 degree intervals around the outer cylindrical surface of boss (14) at a distance of 8 mm from the fastener head seating face. Each gauge has a gauge factor of 2.1 and is excited at 5 V. The station PLC samples all channels at 1 kHz. Target torque is 120 Nm applied at 25 rpm with a final angle of 90 degrees after seating. The corresponding target strain sum is 1850 microstrain. If measured torque is between 116.4 Nm and 123.6 Nm and strain sum is between 1794 and 1906 microstrain the fastener is accepted. The system records both values and the vehicle VIN. If either value falls outside tolerance the PLC issues a command to the nutrunner to add 10 Nm and repeats the measurement once. Persistent failure after the second attempt lights a red beacon and stops the conveyor segment. The calibration curve relating torque to strain was established on ten sample bosses using ultrasonic bolt length measurement accurate to 0.001 mm, confirming that 120 Nm produces 0.085 mm elongation corresponding to the required clamp load. Temperature compensation is applied using a fourth dummy gauge on an unloaded portion of the same sub-frame. The verification cycle adds 4 seconds to station time. The method prevents release of vehicles with under-torqued fasteners without requiring post-assembly service intervention.
